Chapter 1

Chapter 1 — Temperature 0 / 13

Temperature, thermal equilibrium, thermometers, and temperature scales
Q1
Temperature is best described as a measure of the:
✅ Temperature reflects how fast the molecules are vibrating. Heat flows from higher to lower temperature.
Q2
Heat always flows from:
✅ When there is no more temperature difference, there is no more heat transfer.
Q3
The normal oral body temperature is:
✅ Rectal is about 37.3 °C (99.2 °F), and rectal is the most accurate of oral, axillary, and rectal.
Q4
Body temperature is regulated by thermoregulatory centers in the:
✅ The hypothalamus balances heat production and heat loss. About 85% of heat loss is through the skin.
Q5
Hypothermia exists when core temperature falls below:
✅ Fever (pyrexia) is a temperature above normal. Infants are at risk because of poor thermoregulation and high surface-area-to-weight ratio.
Q6
Which temperature measurement is generally the most accurate?
✅ Rectal averages about 1 °F higher than oral and 1.5 °F higher than axillary.
Q7
Two bodies in contact at the same temperature are in:
✅ There is no net exchange of heat. This follows the Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics and is the basis of thermometer readings.
Q8
Mercury equipment, including mercury-in-glass thermometers, was phased out of Philippine hospitals starting:
✅ Mercury content is dangerous to people.
Q9
A non-contact (infrared/laser) thermometer measures temperature using:
✅ Liquid crystal thermometers change color; resistance thermometers use platinum wire.
Q10
A rotary thermometer works because the two joined metals in its coiled bimetallic strip:
✅ The strip bends, moving the pointer along the scale.
Q11
Absolute zero is:
✅ It is the temperature at which matter is said to have lost all its thermal energy.
Q12
On the Fahrenheit scale, water freezes and boils at:
✅ There are 180 divisions between the fixed points. Celsius is 0 and 100, and Kelvin is 273 and 373.
Q13
Convert 27 °C to Kelvin using K = °C + 273:
✅ 27 + 273 = 300 K.
Chapter 2

Chapter 2 — Heat 0 / 12

Sources of heat, thermal expansion, and change of phase
Q1
Heat is best defined as:
✅ The caloric fluid theory was disproved by Count Rumford when boring a cannon barrel.
Q2
Rubbing your hands together to warm them is an example of heat from:
✅ Other sources: bending metal and collision are also mechanical in nature.
Q3
Heat used to turn water into steam to run a turbine comes from which artificial source?
✅ The sun and the interior of the earth are the natural sources.
Q4
A 1-m aluminum rod (α = 24 × 10⁻⁶/°C) is heated from 30 °C to 50 °C. Its change in length ΔL = αL₀ΔT is:
✅ ΔL = (24 × 10⁻⁶)(1)(20) = 0.00048 m, so the final length is 1.00048 m.
Q5
A 200-m steel bridge (α = 12 × 10⁻⁶/°C) at 20 °C goes to -30 °C. The change in length is:
✅ ΔL = (12 × 10⁻⁶)(200)(-50) = -0.12 m (contraction). At +40 °C it expands 0.048 m.
Q6
The formula for volume expansion of liquids is:
✅ A 72 L tank of gasoline (β = 950 × 10⁻⁶/°C) warmed from 18 °C to 32 °C overflows about 0.96 L.
Q7
Gas volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature at constant pressure. This is:
✅ Temperatures must be in Kelvin.
Q8
250 mL of oxygen at 27 °C is warmed to 35 °C at constant pressure. The new volume is:
✅ V = 250 × 308 / 300 = 256.7 mL.
Q9
Vaporization that takes place at the surface of a liquid is called:
✅ Boiling is vaporization below the surface, forming bubbles.
Q10
The change of state from gas to liquid is:
✅ Freezing is liquid to solid, and fusion (melting) is solid to liquid.
Q11
A liquid has:
✅ It takes the shape of its container. Solids have both, and gases have neither.
Q12
Generally, when an object’s temperature increases, its molecules move more rapidly and the object:
✅ Thermal expansion. Most substances expand when heated and contract when cooled.
Chapter 3

Chapter 3 — Heat Capacity 0 / 13

Heat capacity, specific heat, mixtures, latent heat, and heat transfer
Q1
Heat capacity is the quantity of heat necessary to:
✅ Specific heat is for 1 gram of a substance.
Q2
One calorie is equal to:
✅ 1 kcal = 1,000 cal. The joule is the SI unit for heat.
Q3
The specific heat of water is:
✅ It is 4.18 kJ/kg·K. Water is hard to heat up or cool down.
Q4
How much heat is needed to raise 200 g of iron (c = 0.11 cal/g·°C) from 27 °C to 80 °C?
✅ H = mcΔT = 200 × 0.11 × 53 = 1,166 cal.
Q5
Heat needed to raise 5 kg of water by 10 K (c = 4180 J/kg·K):
✅ H = 5 × 4180 × 10 = 209,000 J.
Q6
In the method of mixtures, assuming no heat loss:
✅ mcΔT (given off) = mcΔT (absorbed).
Q7
100 g of iron (c = 0.11) cools from 100 °C to 60 °C. The heat it gives off is:
✅ H = 100 × 0.11 × 40 = 440 cal, equal to the heat absorbed by 22 g of water warming from 40 °C to 60 °C.
Q8
The heat of fusion of ice is:
✅ It is the heat needed to change 1 g of solid to liquid without a temperature change (335 kJ/kg).
Q9
Heat needed to melt 25 g of ice at 0 °C into water at 0 °C:
✅ H = mh_f = 25 × 80 = 2,000 cal (2.0 kcal).
Q10
Latent heat is called “hidden” because:
✅ Heat absorbed during a phase change does not change the temperature.
Q11
The heat of vaporization of water is about:
✅ H_v = mh_v. For 10 g of water, that is 5,400 cal.
Q12
Metals are good conductors of heat because they contain:
✅ Conduction is transfer by direct contact. Wood, cork, and polystyrene are insulators.
Q13
Heat from the sun reaches the earth mainly by:
✅ Radiation travels as electromagnetic waves through empty space. Convection happens only in fluids.

Chapter 4 — Thermal Properties of Matter 0 / 12

Equation of state, gas laws, and the kinetic-molecular model
Q1
An equation of state relates:
✅ Pressure, volume, temperature, and amount of substance are the state variables.
Q2
In pV = nRT, the value of R in SI units is:
✅ R = 0.08206 L·atm/mol·K when using atm and liters.
Q3
One mole of ideal gas at STP (273.15 K, 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa) occupies about:
✅ V = nRT/p ≈ 22.4 L.
Q4
Boyle’s Law states that, at constant temperature and moles of gas:
✅ P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. The graph is hyperbolic.
Q5
Helium at 800 torr occupies 0.250 L. The pressure when volume becomes 500 mL is:
✅ P₂ = 800 × 250 / 500 = 400 torr.
Q6
Charles’s Law requires temperature to be expressed in:
✅ V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂. Convert °C to K by adding 273.
Q7
A gas in a fixed container is at 125 atm and 20 °C. Its pressure at 35 °C is:
✅ Gay-Lussac’s Law: P₂ = 125 × 308 / 293 = 131.40 atm.
Q8
A mixture of N₂, CO₂, and O₂ has total pressure 150 kPa. If N₂ = 100 kPa and CO₂ = 24 kPa, the partial pressure of O₂ is:
✅ Dalton’s Law: P_total = P₁ + P₂ + … so 150 - 124 = 26 kPa.
Q9
Gases with partial pressures of 2.00, 3.00, and 4.00 atm are in one container. The total pressure is:
✅ 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 atm.
Q10
A 30.0 mL bubble at 4.5 atm and 15 °C rises to 1.0 atm and 20 °C. Its new volume (combined gas law) is about:
✅ V₂ = (4.5 × 30 × 293) / (288 × 1) = 137.34 mL.
Q11
Avogadro’s number, the number of molecules in a mole, is:
✅ M = N_A × m relates molar mass to the mass of one molecule.
Q12
In the kinetic-molecular model of an ideal gas, molecules collide with the container walls in:
✅ Molecules are point particles in constant motion and the walls are rigid.
